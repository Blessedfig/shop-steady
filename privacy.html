Privacy

Last updated November 28, 2026. Downtown Stube is in private testing. This notice says plainly what the website and the app collect, why, who else handles it, and how long it is kept. It will be reviewed by a lawyer before Downtown Stube is offered to other businesses.

Who we are

Downtown Stube is made by The Blessed Fig Tree, an independent bookstore in Antigo, Wisconsin. Questions or requests: owner@theblessedfigtree.com.

Two kinds of information

Your store's information. When a business uses the app, the business decides what goes in it: its team, schedules, time clock, pay rates, customers, stock and books. The business is in charge of that information. We store it for the business and use it only to run the app for them. We do not sell it, rent it or use it for advertising.

Information about visitors and testers. The sign-in email for each person a business invites, and messages you send us.

What the app holds

  • Sign-in: email address, a password (stored scrambled by our sign-in provider, never readable by us) and the person's role.
  • Team: names, schedules, clock-in and clock-out times, breaks, pay rates, store PINs (stored only as a one-way scramble), checklists done, and messages to managers (which can be anonymous).
  • HR files (managers and the owner only): start date, emergency contact, which new-hire forms are done, reviews and write-ups. The app is designed to keep out Social Security numbers, bank numbers, ID copies and medical papers. Please don't type them in.
  • Customers: name, email or phone, birthday (month and day), what they like, special orders, notes, and whether they said yes to offers by email.
  • Business records: stock, suppliers, costs, expenses, receipts, mileage and tax-year estimates (the owner only).
  • Photos: a photo is taken only when someone chooses to attach one, for example to a "not done" report or a receipt.

Who else handles it

  • Supabase stores the app's data and runs sign-in.
  • GitHub Pages hosts this website and the app's files. Like any web host, it sees visitors' IP addresses.
  • Google Fonts supplies the typefaces on some pages, so your browser asks Google for them.
  • Anthropic (Claude) powers the optional AI helpers: the Ask assistant, tidying a note, and reading a supplier invoice. When someone uses one, the question or document, plus the store data that person is allowed to see, is sent to Anthropic to get the answer. Anthropic's commercial terms say it does not train its models on this data. Questions and answers are not stored by the app. The owner can leave these helpers switched off.
  • Shopify, if the owner connects the store, sends sales, stock and product data into the app, and the app can update products and stock there.

We don't use advertising or tracking cookies. The app keeps a copy of your store's data in your own browser so it works when the internet drops.

How long it is kept

  • Time clock records and payroll figures: at least 3 years, as federal and Wisconsin law require.
  • Everything else: until the business deletes it, or within 60 days after the business closes its account, except backups, which roll off within 90 days.
  • Customers can ask the store to see, fix or delete what it holds about them, and to stop offers.

Staff

Wisconsin law lets employees look at their personnel records. Ask your manager or the owner. Messages you post anonymously in Team voice are saved without your name.

Children

The app is for businesses and their staff, not for children. A store may record a working teen's hours so the schedule follows child labor rules.

Security

Sign-in is invite only, each role sees only its own tools, and private areas such as pay, HR files and the books are limited in the database itself. No system is perfect. If something goes wrong, we will tell the affected business promptly so it can meet its own notice duties, including Wisconsin's (Wis. Stat. 134.98).

Changes

When this notice changes, the date at the top changes too. Big changes will be emailed to each business using the app.
