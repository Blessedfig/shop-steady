Terms for testers

Last updated November 28, 2026. These short terms cover the private test of Downtown Stube. They will be replaced by full terms, reviewed by a lawyer, before Downtown Stube is sold.

It's a test

Downtown Stube is free during testing. It may change, break, lose data or go away. Keep your own copies of anything important, such as payroll and tax records, and use the export buttons often. The app is provided as it is, without promises that it fits any purpose.

Not legal, tax, payroll or financial advice

The app's notes on overtime, breaks, child labor, hiring, write-offs, depreciation and taxes are general information to help you get organized. They can be out of date and don't know your situation. Check pay and tax questions with your accountant, payroll provider or lawyer, and the Wisconsin Department of Workforce Development. You are responsible for paying your team correctly and filing your taxes.

Your data

Your business's information belongs to your business. You can download it at any time. We use it only to run the app for you, as the privacy notice explains. You are responsible for having the right to put your team's and customers' information into the app, and for telling them how you use it.

Use it fairly

Don't use the app to break the law, to collect information people didn't agree to share, to send texts or emails people didn't sign up for, or to try to get into another business's data. Don't type Social Security numbers, bank or card numbers, ID copies or medical information into it.

AI helpers

AI answers and AI-read invoices can be wrong. A person checks before anything counts, and nothing the assistant says changes your records.

Connected services

Shopify, Faire, publishers and other services you connect have their own terms. Downtown Stube uses only their official connections and never stores your passwords for them.

Limits

As far as the law allows, The Blessed Fig Tree isn't responsible for lost profits, lost data, or indirect damages from using the test app. Wisconsin law governs these terms.
